Privacy policy

Court Rhythm website and app. Version 1.0, 25 October 2026.

The short version

  • The website uses no cookies, no analytics and no advertising trackers.
  • The app uses your location only while it is open, to check whether you are at a court. The position is checked on our server and then discarded. We never keep a location history.
  • Other players see a count of people at a court. Your nickname and skill level are shown only when at least three people are active at the same court. Your age is never shown.
  • App data is stored in the European Union. We do not sell personal data.
  • You can see, export, correct and delete your data, and withdraw consent at any time.

1. Who is responsible for your data

The controller of your personal data is BEMZ Europe sp. z o.o., ul. Borowej Góry 6 lok. 2, 01-354 Warsaw, Poland, entered in the register of entrepreneurs of the National Court Register (KRS) under number 0001192219, NIP 5223346100, REGON 542640518 ("we", "us"). Court Rhythm is our product.

For anything about your data, write to contact@courtrhythm.com or by post to the address above. We have not appointed a data protection officer. If we do, we will publish their contact details here.

2. What this policy covers

This policy explains how we process personal data when you:

  • visit courtrhythm.com (the "website");
  • write to us by email; and
  • use the Court Rhythm mobile app for Android (the "app"); this policy will also cover the iPhone version when it is released.

We process personal data under the General Data Protection Regulation (EU) 2016/679 ("GDPR", in Polish "RODO") and the Polish Act of 10 May 2018 on the protection of personal data.

3. The website

Hosting and server logs

When you open the website, your browser sends technical data to the server that hosts it: your IP address, the date and time, the page requested, your browser and operating system, and the referring page. The website is currently hosted on GitHub Pages (GitHub, Inc.). GitHub may record this data in server logs to deliver the site and keep it secure. We do not use these logs to identify you. We plan to move hosting to OVH (OVH Sp. z o.o., Poland); this policy will be updated when we do.

Legal basis: our legitimate interest in running a secure website (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

Cookies and analytics

The website currently sets no cookies and uses no analytics, tracking pixels or advertising tools. Fonts and images are served from our own website, not from third-party font or image services.

If we later introduce cookies or similar technologies (such as browser local storage) that are not strictly necessary, for example for analytics or advertising, we will ask for your consent before they are set. We will list each one with its purpose and duration, and update this policy. You can withdraw your consent at any time. Strictly necessary cookies, if any, do not require consent and will also be listed.

Links to social media

The website links to our profiles on Facebook, Instagram and LinkedIn. These are plain links: nothing is loaded from those platforms until you click one. Once you visit a platform, its own privacy policy applies.

4. When you email us

If you write to us, we process your email address, your name if you give it, and the content of your message, to answer you and handle your request. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in replying (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). If your message is about a contract, partnership or a request under the GDPR, we may also rely on Art. 6(1)(b) or 6(1)(c) GDPR. We keep correspondence for as long as needed to deal with the matter and then for up to 3 years in case of follow-up questions or claims, unless the law requires longer. Emails sent to our contact address are handled in a Google account (Gmail), where we also record issue reports in a spreadsheet (Google Sheets) with the sender, the subject and an excerpt. We use an AI assistant (Claude, provided by Anthropic) to help sort and summarise incoming emails. To do this, it may receive your email address, your name if you give it, and the content of your message.

5. The app

You must be 16 or older to create an account. Before you start, the app asks you to accept the Terms of Service and this policy, and separately asks for permission to use your location. Each consent is recorded together with the version of the text you agreed to.

5.1 Your account

  • Email address, used to sign in with a one-time link or code. If you choose "Sign in with Google" or, when an iPhone version is released, "Sign in with Apple", we receive your email address and an account identifier from Google or Apple. When Sign in with Apple becomes available with an iPhone version, Apple lets you hide your real address; we then receive a relay address.
  • Profile: nickname, your sports, skill level for each sport, and your year of birth. We store your year of birth only to check that you are 16 or over. Your age is never shown to other users, not even as a range, and never returned by our systems to them. Your nickname is checked automatically against a list of blocked words when you set or change it.
  • Consent records: what you agreed to, which version, and when.

Legal basis: performance of the contract to provide the app (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR); for consent records, our legal obligation to be able to prove consent (Art. 6(1)(c) and Art. 7(1) GDPR).

5.2 Location

Location is optional. With your permission, while the map is open and while you have an active session, the app sends your device's position to our server. The server checks whether you are at a court and returns a yes or no. The position itself is then discarded.

  • We never store your coordinates and never send them to another user's device.
  • We do not keep a location history.
  • The app only asks for location "while using the app". It never asks for background location.

Without location permission you can still browse the map and see how busy courts are. You can't check in. You can withdraw location consent at any time in the app's settings or in your phone's settings.

Legal basis: your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR).

5.3 Check-ins and sessions

When you check in, we record that you were at a particular court, for which sport, and for how long. While you are checked in, other signed-in users can see:

  • how many people are active at that court, and you are part of that number;
  • your nickname and skill level for that sport, when at least three people are active at the same court. Below three, only the count is shown.

Other users never see your exact position, your age, your email address or where you have been before.

Session records stay linked to your account for 60 days. During that time we use them to show you your own recent activity and to count how many different players use a court (on the map and in search). After 60 days the link to you is removed; anonymous counts stay: we remove your account, skill level and purpose from the record and round the time to the hour, so only the court, the sport and the hour remain. These anonymous counts show when courts are usually busy and how much a court is used month by month. If we add a personal check-in history later, we will ask you in the app first; it will be off unless you turn it on.

Legal basis: performance of the contract (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR). Busy-time statistics: our legitimate interest in providing useful, aggregated court information (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

5.4 Favourites, court information and safety

  • Your courts: courts you mark as favourite or hide.
  • Court information: confirmations and corrections you send about a court, for example which sports it has, whether it exists, whether it is publicly accessible, or whether it is in usable condition. We store who sent the contribution so we can prevent abuse and handle questions. Some reports about a court can change the court's status automatically, for example to hidden, restricted or unsafe.
  • Blocking: players you have blocked, so we can hide them from you.
  • Court photos: court photos are taken by our team only, with only buildings in the frame. The app has no access to your pictures.
  • Reporting players: if you report a player, we store who reported whom, the court, the reason you chose and any detail you add (up to 500 characters). Only our admins see reports. The reported player is never told who reported them.

Legal basis: performance of the contract (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR) and our legitimate interest in keeping court data accurate and the app safe (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). For player reports, also our obligations as a hosting service under the EU Digital Services Act (Art. 6(1)(c) GDPR).

5.5 The map and address search

Map images (tiles) are loaded from OpenFreeMap. To deliver them, OpenFreeMap receives your device's IP address and the requested map area. It does not receive your account details. Court data is based on OpenStreetMap (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL) and public sources.

Address search goes through our server. The app sends the text you type and the current map view to our server, which forwards them to Photon (komoot GmbH, Germany) and Nominatim (OpenStreetMap Foundation, United Kingdom); each serves as the fallback for the other. Your device's IP address and your account details are not forwarded; these services see requests from our server.

Legal basis: performance of the contract (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

When you open a court that has no address yet, our server looks one up in public address data. To prevent abuse, we record which account asked for which court and when, and delete this record after 7 days. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in protecting the service from abuse (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

Your last few searches are kept only on your device, so you can repeat them. They are never sent to us and are cleared when you sign out or delete your account.

5.6 Features that come later

Some features are planned but not yet available. We will update this policy, and ask for consent where needed, before they go live:

  • Push notifications, through Firebase Cloud Messaging (Google). This will need a device token and will be off by default, with a separate opt-in for each type of alert. Notifications will never show message text or who sent it.
  • Chat for players at a court. Only your nickname will be shown.
  • Photos uploaded by users. In the first release, only court photos taken by our team are shown, with location data removed from each file.

5.7 Technical and security data

Our servers record technical events, such as sign-in attempts, errors and the IP address of requests, to run the service, find faults and protect against abuse. Supabase also stores the IP address and device information of each sign-in session for as long as the session lasts, until it ends by sign-out or expiry. Coordinates and sign-in tokens are never written to these logs. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in a secure, working service (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

  • The team's admins have access to admin tools.
  • App store reviewers use test accounts.

7. Transfers outside the European Economic Area

Some of our providers are based in the USA or may process data there. When personal data is transferred outside the EEA, we rely on a European Commission adequacy decision (for the USA, the EU–US Data Privacy Framework, for companies certified under it; for the United Kingdom, the Commission's adequacy decisions for the UK) or on the European Commission's standard contractual clauses. You can ask us for information about these safeguards and for a copy of them.

9. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to:

  • access your data and get a copy of it (Art. 15);
  • correct inaccurate data (Art. 16);
  • delete your data (Art. 17). In the app, you can delete your account yourself in the settings;
  • restrict processing (Art. 18);
  • data portability: receive your data in a machine-readable format (Art. 20). The app has a data export;
  • object to processing based on our legitimate interest (Art. 21);
  • withdraw consent at any time, without affecting processing before you withdrew (Art. 7(3)); and
  • lodge a complaint with the supervisory authority: the President of the Personal Data Protection Office (Prezes Urzędu Ochrony Danych Osobowych), ul. Stawki 2, 00-193 Warsaw, uodo.gov.pl.

In the app you can download a copy of the personal data we hold in your account, such as your profile, your activity and the choices you made in the app. Some information, for example reports that other people made about you, is not part of the export.

To use your rights, write to contact@courtrhythm.com. We will answer within one month. If we need to confirm who you are, we will ask, for example to reply from the email address linked to your account.

10. Automated decisions and profiling

We don't make decisions about you based solely on automated processing that have legal or similarly significant effects, and we don't profile you for advertising.

11. Age limit

Court Rhythm is for people aged 16 and over. If we learn that an account belongs to someone younger, we delete it. If you think a child under 16 has given us personal data, please contact us.

12. Security

We protect personal data with technical and organisational measures appropriate to the risk, including encrypted connections (HTTPS), access rules on every database table so each user can reach only their own data, no secrets stored in the app, minimal data collection, and limited access for our team.

13. Do you have to give us your data?

Giving data is voluntary. Without an email address you can't create an account, and without location permission you can't check in at a court.

14. Changes to this policy

We will update this policy when our services or the law change. We will publish the new version here with a new date. If a change affects what you consented to, the app will ask you to agree again.
